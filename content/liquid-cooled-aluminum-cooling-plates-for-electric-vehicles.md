# Liquid-Cooled Aluminum Cooling Plates for Electric Vehicles

Liquid-cooled aluminum plates and housings are used in battery, inverter and power-electronics thermal management. The manufacturing challenge is not simply creating a metal plate: the design must consider flow, pressure, sealing, distortion, machining, cleaning and a validated inspection plan.

## Start with the Thermal and Mechanical Requirement

Before selecting a manufacturing route, define:

- Heat load and duty cycle
- Coolant chemistry
- Inlet temperature and flow rate
- Maximum operating pressure
- Pressure-cycle and thermal-cycle requirements
- Permitted pressure drop
- Leak limit and test method
- Structural and mounting loads
- Interfaces to cells, modules, power devices or housings
- Service and maintenance requirements

These inputs determine whether a cast plate, machined plate, bonded assembly or hybrid route should be evaluated. A supplier cannot validate thermal performance from a drawing alone.

## Aluminum Alloy Selection

Alloy selection depends on the process route and required properties. Review:

- Casting or forming capability
- Thermal conductivity
- Strength and elongation
- Machinability
- Corrosion behavior with the coolant
- Weldability, brazing or bonding compatibility
- Surface-treatment requirements

A356 and ADC12 may be discussed for different aluminum component concepts, but neither is a universal cooling-plate answer. Their process, heat-treatment and joining implications differ. [Compare A356 and ADC12 for demanding structural parts](/why-we-recommended-a356-over-adc12-high-stress-structural-parts/).

## Cooling-Plate Design and Flow Paths

The flow path should be developed with the thermal model and manufacturing method together. Important features include:

- Channel width, depth and cross-section
- Supply and return port positions
- Baffles and flow distribution
- Dead zones and air traps
- Wall thickness around channels
- Sealing lands and fastener locations
- Machining exposure after casting or joining

Sharp corners, abrupt section changes and isolated masses can affect filling, distortion and service life. DFM should review these areas before tooling or process release. [Review casting DFM and tooling](/die-casting-tooling/).

## Die-Casting Feasibility

Aluminum HPDC may be considered when the geometry, volume and performance targets fit the process. Relevant questions include:

- Can the flow paths be formed reliably without unacceptable turbulence or thin sections?
- Can the plate be ejected without excessive distortion?
- Where will machining expose internal conditions?
- What pressure-tightness will be validated?
- Is a vacuum-assist or other process control justified for the risk?
- What acceptance criteria apply to critical seal areas?

The casting route must be supported by a project-specific tool, process and validation plan. A nominal machine tonnage does not prove feasibility for a particular plate.

## Porosity, Sealing and Leak Testing

Coolant leakage can originate from connected porosity, cracks, joint defects, sealing interfaces or machining damage. The manufacturing plan should separate these risks:

- **Internal casting quality:** controlled through alloy, filling, thermal balance and process stability.
- **Joining quality:** validated through the selected welding, brazing or bonding process.
- **Machining quality:** controlled through tooling, fixtures, surface finish and edge quality.
- **Sealing performance:** confirmed through a functional pressure or leak test.

Potential test methods include pressure decay, mass-flow leak testing, helium testing, burst testing or other approved methods. The choice depends on sensitivity, geometry, production stage and customer requirements.

No process guarantees zero leakage. Define the test medium, pressure, stabilization time, temperature, leak limit, fixture, coverage and acceptance rule. [Review porosity and X-ray planning](/porosity-control-x-ray-inspection-castings/).

## CNC Machining and Flatness

After casting or forming, cooling plates may require [CNC machining](/precision-cnc-machining/) for:

- Sealing faces
- Ports and threaded connections
- Mounting holes
- Sensor or busbar interfaces
- Flatness and profile requirements

The fixture plan should avoid introducing distortion during clamping. Machining sequence, cutting loads, coolant and in-process inspection can affect the final result.

Flatness, profile and position tolerances must be specified feature by feature. A blanket tolerance should not be applied to every surface.

## Cleaning, Finishing and Assembly

Residual chips, coolant and manufacturing residue can affect flow and long-term reliability. Define:

- Internal cleanliness standard
- Cleaning method and verification
- Dryness before sealing
- Surface treatment and masking
- Coating thickness and electrical-contact areas
- Protection of sealing interfaces
- Packaging and contamination control

Surface finishing may include conversion coating, e-coating or another approved system. Material compatibility and electrical grounding should be reviewed with the battery or power-electronics interface. [Review finishing considerations](/surface-finishing-aluminum-magnesium-casting/).

## Inspection and Qualification

The approved plan may include:

- Material and process records
- First-article dimensional inspection
- Critical-channel or seal-area inspection
- Pressure or leak testing
- Flow testing
- Surface and coating checks
- Cleanliness verification
- Burst or pressure-cycle testing where required

The report format, traceability, sampling and acceptance criteria should be agreed before production. These requirements should not be inferred from a general quality-system claim.

- 3D model and 2D drawing with GD&T

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can aluminum cooling plates be produced by die casting?

Die casting may be considered for suitable plate or housing geometries, but it is not automatically the right route. Flow-path layout, channel geometry, wall sections, volume, sealing and validation requirements must be reviewed against die casting, machining, brazing or assembly alternatives.

### How do you prevent leaks in liquid-cooled aluminum plates?

Leak risk is managed through design, process capability, machining and a validated test plan. Define the coolant, pressure, temperature, leak limit, fixture and acceptance method before production; no manufacturing process guarantees zero leakage.

### Is X-ray inspection enough for a cooling plate?

Not by itself. X-ray can provide useful internal information but may not detect every leak path. Pressure-decay, flow, dimensional, metallurgical and functional tests provide different evidence and may be used together.

### What causes warpage after machining?

Casting stress, uneven material removal, fixture clamping, thermal exposure and thin sections can contribute. The process plan should address datum strategy, machining sequence, stress relief if specified, coolant conditions and dimensional verification.

### What should an EV cooling-plate RFQ include?

Provide the drawing, alloy, coolant and operating conditions, pressure and leak limits, flow-path requirements, sealing interfaces, surface finish, machining features, volume, validation plan and documentation scope.
