# Aluminum to Magnesium Conversion: A Weight-Reduction Guide

Magnesium alloys can reduce component mass because their density is lower than aluminum, but an achievable reduction is not a fixed percentage. A successful conversion is a redesign and validation project involving stiffness, strength, joints, corrosion and manufacturing.

## Why “33% Lighter” Is Not a Design Guarantee

A same-volume magnesium part may be lighter than an equivalent aluminum volume because magnesium has lower density. The finished component can also require different walls, ribs, bosses, inserts or attachments.

The actual mass outcome depends on:

- Strength and stiffness targets
- Load paths and attachment points
- Fatigue and impact requirements
- Minimum manufacturing sections
- Screw threads and inserts
- Corrosion allowance
- Coating and galvanic isolation
- Validation and safety factors

The correct target is the lowest validated mass that satisfies the complete requirement, not a fixed percentage copied from a generic comparison.

## Magnesium and Aluminum Density

Magnesium is used for lightweight structural applications because its density is lower than aluminum and steel. However, density alone does not determine final part mass.

Compare:

- Elastic modulus
- Yield and tensile strength
- Elongation and impact behavior
- Fatigue performance
- Thermal expansion
- Creep and temperature limits
- Machining and finishing requirements

For an apple-to-apple study, compare the redesigned magnesium part with the complete aluminum assembly, including inserts, fasteners, coatings and any added reinforcement. [Review magnesium die casting services](/magnesium-die-casting-services/).

## AZ91D and AM60B Selection

AZ91D and AM60B are common magnesium die-casting alloys with different selection tendencies:

| Alloy | Typical selection focus | Important checks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AZ91D | Strength-oriented housings, brackets and covers | Corrosion system, ductility, impact and temperature |
| AM60B | Impact- or ductility-oriented components | Strength, fatigue, casting quality and validation method |

These are starting points, not universal rankings. Review the [AZ91D automotive guide](/az91d-magnesium-die-casting-automotive-parts/) and [AM60B supplier guide](/am60b-magnesium-alloy-die-casting-suppliers/) for process and qualification questions.

## Stiffness and Wall Thickness

Magnesium has a lower elastic modulus than aluminum. A direct wall reduction may therefore increase deflection even when the part weighs less.

The redesign review should consider:

- Rib and web layout
- Load paths
- Gusset and boss locations
- Buckling resistance
- Local contact and bearing stress
- Fastener clamp load
- NVH and natural frequency
- Casting fill and solidification limits

A mass-reduction target should be released only after stiffness and strength are verified for the new geometry.

## Die-Casting Design

Magnesium die casting brings process-specific design considerations:

- Controlled wall transitions
- Draft and ejection
- Gate and overflow layout
- Die thermal balance
- Thin-section flow distance
- Local shrinkage masses
- Core and slider access
- Machining allowance

Flow simulation and DFM can challenge the design before tooling, but the result must be validated with the approved alloy, tool and process. [Review die casting tooling and DFM](/die-casting-tooling/).

## Corrosion and Galvanic Isolation

Magnesium can corrode when exposed to moisture, salts or incompatible materials. Conversion designs must address:

- Drainage and water retention
- Edges and coating coverage
- Steel bolts, inserts and adjacent aluminum parts
- Washer or isolation strategy
- Stone-chip and abrasion exposure
- Cleaning and pretreatment
- Coatings and repair of damaged areas

An approved coating system must be tested on the actual alloy and geometry. A generic salt-spray claim is not a complete design specification. [Review aluminum and magnesium finishing](/surface-finishing-aluminum-magnesium-casting/).

## Machining, Fastening and Assembly

Magnesium has different machining, chip-handling and fire-safety considerations from aluminum. The project plan should cover:

- Approved coolant and cleaning media
- Chip and fines management
- Corrosion protection between operations
- Fixture pressure and thin-wall distortion
- Thread and insert design
- Galvanic isolation
- Torque and joint validation

Assembly requirements should be reviewed before casting geometry and tooling are frozen.

## Validation Plan

A credible conversion plan includes:

1. Define target mass, stiffness, strength and durability.
2. Compare candidate magnesium alloys and process routes.
3. Redesign sections, ribs, bosses and interfaces.
4. Review tooling, casting and machining feasibility.
5. Qualify corrosion protection and galvanic isolation.
6. Validate the casting process and critical internal quality.
7. Perform dimensional, mechanical and application-specific testing.
8. Confirm production control and inspection requirements.

The comparison should include total mass, cost, reliability and service requirements rather than only the raw casting weight.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a magnesium conversion always 33% lighter?

No. Magnesium alloys have lower density than aluminum alloys, but the finished part may not achieve a fixed percentage reduction. Stiffness, strength, wall sections, ribs, inserts, fasteners, manufacturing limits and safety factors all affect the redesign.

### Can magnesium replace aluminum without changing the drawing?

It should not be treated as a drop-in material substitution. The part must be reviewed for strength, stiffness, fatigue, impact, corrosion, galvanic interfaces, machining, coating and validation requirements.

### Can AZ91D and AM60B both be considered?

Yes. AZ91D and AM60B have different selection tendencies, with AZ91D often strength-oriented and AM60B often considered for greater ductility. The approved material specification and test plan determine the final choice.

### How is magnesium corrosion controlled?

Corrosion control combines part design, alloy selection, drainage, dissimilar-metal isolation, surface preparation and an approved coating system. Coating tests must represent the actual alloy, geometry and service environment.

### What information is needed for a conversion review?

Provide the current aluminum design, application, loads, stiffness targets, operating environment, interfaces, expected volume, finishing plan, validation method and the target mass reduction.
