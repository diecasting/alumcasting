# Thixocasting vs Rheocasting: Process Selection for EV Components

Thixocasting and rheocasting are semi-solid metal (SSM) routes, but they are not interchangeable selections made from a generic feature table. The right process depends on the alloy, component geometry, feedstock supply, equipment, tooling, heat-treatment plan and agreed validation method.

## Quick Comparison

| Decision area | Thixocasting | Rheocasting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slurry source | Reheated semi-solid billet | Semi-solid slurry prepared from liquid alloy |
| Feedstock control | Billet specification, reheating and handling | Melt preparation, slurry generation and transfer |
| Tooling review | Fill, thermal balance, ejection and distortion for the reheated slug | Fill, thermal balance, slurry consistency and cycle control |
| Heat treatment | Must be qualified for the alloy and casting condition | Must be qualified for the alloy and casting condition |
| Cost drivers | Billet supply, reheating energy and handling | Melt preparation, slurry equipment, yield and cycle control |
| Project fit | Depends on the approved alloy, part and supply chain | Depends on the approved alloy, part and production scope |

This table describes process differences, not a universal ranking.

## What Both Processes Have in Common

Both routes aim to shape metal in a partially solid state so the material can flow with less turbulence than a fully liquid casting process. Both require control of:

- Alloy chemistry and melt quality
- The fraction of solid in the slurry
- Slurry temperature and consistency
- Filling velocity, gate design and venting
- Die temperature and thermal balance
- Pressure during solidification
- Ejection, distortion and dimensional stability
- Sampling, inspection and acceptance criteria

An SSM label does not replace part-specific process validation. [Review defect controls before inspection](/porosity-control-x-ray-inspection-castings/).

## Thixocasting Process Principles

Thixocasting normally starts with a specially prepared solid billet that can be reheated into a semi-solid condition. The reheated material is transferred to the casting machine and formed in the die.

The project review should establish:

- The approved billet source and chemistry
- Reheating method and temperature uniformity
- Material handling between reheating and the shot
- Slug geometry and its effect on die filling
- Equipment capability for the planned cycle
- Repeatability of the semi-solid fraction

The feedstock supply chain can add constraints compared with slurry prepared from liquid alloy, but actual cost and availability must be quoted for the selected material and route.

## Rheocasting Process Principles

Rheocasting prepares semi-solid slurry from liquid alloy and transfers it into the casting process. Established production methods use different slurry-generation techniques, so the equipment description must be specific to the proposed route.

The project review should establish:

- Alloy chemistry and melt treatment
- Slurry-generation method and control window
- Transfer time and temperature loss
- Consistency from shot to shot
- Interaction between slurry condition and die filling
- Equipment and consumable requirements

Preparing slurry directly from liquid alloy can shorten the solid-billet supply path, but that does not by itself prove lower total cost, higher quality or broader alloy capability.

## Heat Treatment and Mechanical Properties

SSM processing may create a microstructure that is considered for high-integrity or heat-treated aluminum components. The decision to use T6 or another condition still depends on the customer's material specification and a validated process.

Heat treatment can cause blistering, distortion or property variation when gas content, surface condition and thermal parameters are not controlled. For that reason:

- A T6 condition must be specified for the alloy and part.
- The casting route and heat-treatment cycle must be validated together.
- Mechanical properties should be verified from the required test samples and lot records.
- Surface quality and dimensional effects must be checked after heat treatment.

Do not select an SSM process solely because one brochure claims universal T6 compatibility.

## Component Geometry and Tooling

Suitable SSM candidates often require careful evaluation of:

- Wall-section transitions and flow length
- Heavy masses that may feed shrinkage
- Ribs, bosses and local features
- Datum strategy and machining allowance
- Ejection marks and distortion risk
- Gate, runner and overflows
- Die thermal control
- Feature access for later CNC operations

Thin sections and large projections should be reviewed with the material, process window and validated equipment together. A nominal wall thickness is not a reliable capability statement without these inputs.

## Cost Comparison Must Be Quoted

The cost gap between thixocasting and rheocasting cannot be reduced to a generic percentage. Compare:

- Feedstock price and supplier lead time
- Melting or reheating energy
- Material yield and process scrap
- Machine cycle and projected utilization
- Tooling and maintenance scope
- Heat-treatment and machining allowances
- Inspection and documentation requirements
- Freight and inventory for the chosen supply chain

The lower-cost route may change when volume, alloy, part yield or supplier location changes.

## Porosity, Leakage and Inspection

Neither process guarantees zero porosity or zero leakage. Potential gas and shrinkage conditions must be evaluated using a process-specific plan.

- **X-ray or radiography** can reveal selected internal conditions but depends on geometry, thickness, defect type and orientation.
- **Pressure or leak testing** measures a functional response against the approved limit but does not identify every internal defect.
- **Metallographic or mechanical testing** provides different evidence about microstructure and properties.

Inspection method, critical areas, sampling, acceptance limits and documentation must be agreed before production. [Read the inspection-planning guide](/porosity-control-x-ray-inspection-castings/).

## How to Select the Process

A sound selection review starts with the requirement rather than the process name:

1. Define the part function, alloy and heat-treatment condition.
2. Identify critical strength, ductility, sealing and dimensional features.
3. Review geometry, volume and tooling constraints.
4. Confirm which equipment and supplier route have been qualified.
5. Compare total cost and lead time using project quotations.
6. Define process validation, first-article and production inspection.
7. Verify property and quality records against the approved plan.

For aluminum structural projects, reviewing material behavior is part of that decision. Read [A356 versus ADC12 for high-stress structural parts](/why-we-recommended-a356-over-adc12-high-stress-structural-parts/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is thixocasting better than rheocasting?

Neither process is universally better. The appropriate route depends on alloy, feedstock supply, part geometry, tooling, equipment, process control, heat-treatment requirements, volume and validation plan.

### What is the main feedstock difference?

Thixocasting normally reheats a specially prepared solid billet, while rheocasting prepares semi-solid slurry from liquid alloy. Each route creates different material handling and process-control requirements.

### Can semi-solid castings be heat treated?

Heat treatment may be evaluated for a suitable alloy and process route, but it is not automatically safe for every casting. Internal gas, surface condition, distortion and alloy response must be validated before production.

### Does semi-solid casting guarantee zero porosity?

No. Semi-solid processing can change filling and solidification behavior, but it does not eliminate every source of gas or shrinkage porosity. Acceptance must be defined by the drawing, process plan and inspection method.

### What should an SSM project RFQ include?

Provide the alloy specification, 3D model, 2D drawing with GD&T, component function, wall sections, annual volume, heat-treatment condition, inspection requirements and any approved equipment or supplier constraints.
