Gravity die casting can be a strong process choice for aluminum parts that need slower, controlled mold filling, thicker sections or a heat-treatable route. It is not automatically stronger or more accurate than other casting processes; the correct decision depends on the drawing, alloy, volume, tooling and acceptance requirements.

This guide explains how to review a **gravity die casting manufacturer** for an aluminum component and compare GDC with sand casting, low-pressure casting and high-pressure die casting.

## How Gravity Die Casting Works

Permanent mold casting uses a reusable metal mold. Metal is poured into the cavity under gravity, then solidifies against the mold surfaces.

A typical development sequence is:

1. Drawing and DFM review
2. Alloy and process selection
3. Mold and gating design
4. Pattern, mold and fixture manufacture
5. Trial casting and process qualification
6. Heat treatment where required
7. Trimming, machining and finishing
8. Dimensional and internal-quality inspection

The lower filling energy compared with HPDC can reduce some types of air entrapment, but turbulence, oxide films, shrinkage and gas pickup still require control.

## GDC Versus HPDC and Sand Casting

| Process | Potential fit | Important limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Gravity die casting | Reusable tooling, medium volume, thicker or heat-treated parts | Cycle rate, minimum section, draft and tooling cost |
| Sand casting | Larger or low-volume geometries and flexible design changes | Pattern tooling, surface, repeatability and higher machining stock |
| HPDC | High volume and thin-wall geometry suited to die casting | Thick sections, porosity, high tooling cost and heat-treatment constraints |

GDC is not simply a slower version of HPDC. Fill, solidification, tool design, coating, ejection and quality controls are different. The process should be selected from the part rather than from a preferred supplier label.

## Aluminum Alloy Selection

Material selection drives castability, strength, elongation, corrosion behavior and heat-treatment response. Alloys that may be considered in GDC include:

- A356 for castability and heat-treatable structural applications
- A357 where higher strength or elongation is required and qualified
- A319 for selected elevated-temperature applications
- Other customer-specified alloys subject to availability and process review

Alloy recommendations should not be presented as stock availability. Final selection should consider mechanical requirements, casting design, heat treatment, machining, corrosion protection and validation.

See the [A356 and ADC12 selection comparison](/why-we-recommended-a356-over-adc12-high-stress-structural-parts/) for material-route context.

## T6 Heat Treatment

T6 generally involves solution treatment followed by quenching and artificial aging. For a casting, the review should cover:

- Alloy chemistry and heat-treatment response
- Casting soundness and gas content
- Section thickness and thermal gradients
- Quench method and distortion risk
- Fixturing during quench
- Aging temperature and time
- Mechanical-property and hardness verification
- Blister, crack and dimensional inspection

T6 is not automatically possible for every aluminum casting. The supplier should demonstrate the process on the specified alloy and part, with suitable controls and representative test evidence.

## Tooling and DFM

The permanent mold and auxiliary tooling should be reviewed for:

- Parting line and draft
- Gating and riser strategy
- Mold coating and thermal management
- Cooling or heating
- Feeding and shrinkage control
- Ejection and part handling
- Core and insert access
- Machining allowance and datum stability
- Mold maintenance and expected life basis

Tooling cost and lead time depend on geometry, cores, inserts, finish, cooling design and approval requirements. A quotation should identify the tooling scope rather than relying on a generic schedule.

## Tolerance and Machining

Gravity cast tolerances are feature-specific. The review should consider:

- Raw-cast dimensional requirements
- Draft and mold movement
- Thermal variation and distortion
- Fixture and datum access
- Material condition before machining
- Machining stock and cleanup
- Finished GD&T and surface requirements
- Measurement method and uncertainty

Post-cast CNC machining can refine selected features, but the drawing must support the planned datum and stock strategy. Review [CNC machining for aluminum castings](/precision-cnc-machining/) before assuming a blanket finished tolerance.

## Quality and Inspection

The approved inspection plan may include:

- Chemical composition and material verification
- Heat-treatment records or hardness testing
- Visual and dimensional inspection
- CMM or gauge checks for finished features
- X-ray, CT or sectioning for specified porosity areas
- Pressure or leak testing for sealed components
- Mechanical-property testing when specified
- Surface and coating checks

Inspection methods should match the critical characteristics. A CMM report does not establish internal soundness, and an X-ray image does not replace product-level pressure or functional testing.

## Production and Commercial Review

The supplier review should establish:

- Proposed mold cavitation and cycle
- Required forming, trim, heat-treatment and machining capacity
- Inspection workload and access to required equipment
- Expected first-article and PPAP scope where applicable
- Maintenance and replacement plan for the mold
- Data required for annual-volume commitments
- Change-control process

Installed equipment is not the same as available capacity. The supplier should show how the requested volume fits the actual process route and inspection plan.

## What to Include in a Gravity Die Casting RFQ

Provide:

- 3D model and 2D drawing with GD&T
- Alloy and heat-treatment specification
- Expected annual volume and launch schedule
- Critical structural, sealing and assembly features
- Raw-cast and machined surface requirements
- Machining, finishing and assembly scope
- Inspection methods and documentation
- Customer standards and validation requirements
- Packaging and delivery expectations

Missing requirements can be identified during review, but process capability and commercial scope are confirmed only after the drawing and route are understood.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is gravity die casting?

Gravity die casting, also called permanent mold casting, pours molten aluminum into a reusable metal mold under gravity. It can suit structural or heat-treated parts when the geometry, volume and tooling economics are appropriate.

### When should gravity die casting be compared with HPDC?

GDC may be evaluated for lower volume, thicker sections or heat-treatment-compatible structural parts. HPDC is often considered for higher volume, thinner walls and geometry suited to high-speed die filling.

### Can gravity die castings be T6 heat treated?

Some aluminum alloys and castings can be solution treated and artificially aged, but suitability depends on alloy, casting soundness, geometry, furnace practice and distortion control. T6 compatibility should be qualified for the specific part.

### What tolerance can gravity die casting achieve?

Tolerance depends on the feature, alloy, die condition, draft, thermal control, measurement method and machining allowance. The drawing should be reviewed feature by feature instead of applying a blanket tolerance.

### What should a gravity die casting RFQ include?

Include the drawing, alloy, volume, critical features, heat-treatment condition, machining scope, inspection requirements, finishing and target production timing.
