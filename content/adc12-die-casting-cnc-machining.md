# ADC12 Die Casting and CNC Machining

ADC12 is an aluminum-silicon-copper die-casting alloy commonly considered for housings, covers, brackets and other components that benefit from good castability. AlumCasting reviews the casting design, tooling, post-casting machining and inspection requirements before production.

## ADC12 Alloy Considerations

ADC12 may be evaluated for:

- Electronic and industrial housings.
- Motor covers and machinery components.
- Brackets, covers and mounting parts.
- Other commercial die-cast components with moderate structural requirements.

Final alloy selection should account for strength, operating temperature, corrosion exposure, thermal requirements, machining and production volume. ADC12 is not automatically suitable for every structural or safety-critical application.

## DFM and Tooling Inputs

The DFM review should define:

- 3D model and 2D drawing with GD&T.
- Required alloy and material condition.
- Nominal wall thickness and transitions.
- Draft angles, undercuts and mechanical movements.
- Gating, overflow and thermal-balance considerations.
- Machining allowance, datums and fixture access.
- Sealing features and critical assembly interfaces.
- Expected volume, production stages and inspection scope.

These inputs help identify casting and machining questions before the die design is finalised.

## ADC12 CNC Post-Machining

Casting and machining should be planned together because:

- ADC12 contains hard silicon phases that influence tooling and surface finish.
- Thin-wall parts require controlled clamping and fixture support.
- Machining can expose internal porosity at critical sealing or functional areas.
- Datum selection affects hole, thread, flange and sealing-face accuracy.

The CNC plan should cover machining allowance, fixtures, datum strategy, cutting sequence, critical features and inspection access. Final tolerance requirements are confirmed against the drawing and the approved inspection method.

## Surface Finishing

Machined ADC12 components may require cleaning, conversion coating, powder coating or other finishing to suit the application environment.

The finishing method, appearance requirements and processing source are confirmed during project review and RFQ.

## Inspection and Documentation

Inspection planning is based on the part function and customer requirements. Depending on the approved plan, methods may include:

- X-ray inspection for internal porosity where selected.
- CMM dimensional verification.
- Pressure or leak testing for sealed components.
- Visual and material inspection.
- First-article or production inspection records when specified.

Acceptance criteria, sampling, coverage and report format are agreed before production.

## ADC12 RFQ Requirements

Provide the following for a project review:

- 3D model and 2D drawing with GD&T.
- Aluminum alloy and material condition.
- Expected annual volume and launch timing.
- Critical walls, sealing faces, holes and threads.
- Machining and surface-finishing requirements.
- Inspection, traceability and documentation requirements.

## ADC12 Die Casting FAQ

### What is ADC12 aluminum die casting used for?

ADC12 is an aluminum-silicon-copper die-casting alloy commonly considered for housings, covers, brackets and other complex components that benefit from good castability. Final alloy selection depends on structural, thermal, machining and application requirements.

### What information is needed for ADC12 DFM and RFQ review?

Provide the 3D model, 2D drawing with GD&T, material condition, annual volume, critical walls, draft requirements, machining allowance, sealing features and the required inspection scope.

### Can ADC12 castings be CNC machined after casting?

Yes. Machining allowance, datums, fixture access and finished tolerances should be reviewed with the casting design. Feasibility and inspection requirements are confirmed for the specific cast and machined features.

### How are porosity and inspection requirements controlled?

Acceptable porosity depends on the critical area, drawing requirements, inspection method and agreed sampling plan. X-ray, CMM and pressure or leak testing may be included for the project when specified and approved.
