# Zamak 3 vs Zamak 5: Zinc Die Casting Selection Guide

Zamak 3 and Zamak 5 are both zinc die-casting alloys used for components such as hardware, enclosures, lock parts and small structural assemblies. The right grade depends on strength, hardness, ductility, dimensional stability, finishing, forming operations and the expected service environment.

## The Main Difference: Copper Content

Zamak 3 and Zamak 5 use the same broad zinc-aluminum alloy family, but Zamak 5 contains a higher intentional copper addition. That difference generally gives Zamak 5 higher strength and hardness, while Zamak 3 is often selected where better ductility and dimensional stability are priorities.

The approved material standard and supplier certificate should be checked against the drawing. A generic alloy name does not establish composition, mechanical properties or acceptance for a specific application.

## Where to Get Zamak Property Data

Property searches for Zamak 3 and Zamak 5 often return well-formatted tables without a clear material condition or test basis. Before design release, verify:

- Exact alloy designation and governing standard
- Chemical composition limits
- Density and melting-range basis
- Tensile strength, yield strength, elongation and hardness
- Test method, specimen and casting condition
- Dimensional-aging requirements
- Plating and corrosion-test requirements

These values should be supplied through the approved material specification and inspection plan. They should not be inferred from a generic marketing table or from another supplier's casting.

## Zamak 3: Typical Selection Considerations

Zamak 3 is commonly considered for:

- Complex castings that need good castability
- Components that receive controlled post-casting forming
- Parts where dimensional stability is important
- Decorative hardware and general commercial components
- Applications compatible with common plating and coating systems

Selection depends on the actual stress, temperature, aging and finishing requirements. Zamak 3 should not be treated as universally superior for every thin-wall or long-life part.

## Zamak 5: Typical Selection Considerations

Zamak 5 is commonly evaluated where:

- Higher strength or hardness is useful
- Wear resistance is important
- The part can accept somewhat lower ductility
- Dimensional aging and service exposure have been reviewed
- The material specification supports the proposed finishing route

Zamak 5 is not automatically required for every structural zinc casting. Its selection should be based on the drawing and validated material requirements.

## Zamak Versus Aluminum Die Casting

Zamak is not a drop-in alternative to every aluminum casting. Zinc and aluminum alloys differ in:

- Density and part mass
- Melting and process temperatures
- Castability and thin-detail reproduction
- Corrosion and plating behavior
- Machining and assembly
- Tooling and total part economics

A small zinc component may be competitive where detail, surface finish and high-volume tooling support the route. A larger or weight-sensitive component may favor [aluminum HPDC](/aluminum-die-casting/). Compare the complete part cost, service requirements and finishing route rather than material price per kilogram.

## Selection Comparison

| Requirement | Zamak 3 | Zamak 5 | Design question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength / hardness | Generally lower | Generally higher | Are the loads and wear conditions defined? |
| Ductility / forming | Generally more favorable | Generally less favorable | Will the part be bent, crimped, riveted or staked? |
| Dimensional stability | Often favorable | Requires aging review | How long and under what conditions must dimensions remain stable? |
| Castability | Commonly favorable | Alloy- and part-dependent | Do wall sections and flow distance suit the die? |
| Finishing | Alloy- and process-dependent | Alloy- and process-dependent | Are plating, coating and adhesion requirements approved? |
| Cost / tooling | Project-dependent | Project-dependent | Are volume, scrap, cycle and finishing costs included? |

These are screening considerations, not guaranteed design values. Mechanical data must come from the approved material condition and qualified process.

## DFM for Zinc Die Casting

The alloy choice should be reviewed together with:

- 3D model and drawing with GD&T
- Wall thickness and transitions
- Draft and ejection direction
- Gate, runner and overflow design
- Thermal balance and cooling
- Machining allowance and datum strategy
- Surface finish and plating requirements
- Expected design volume and handling

## Porosity and Inspection

Porosity and surface defects can affect plating, pressure tightness and appearance. The inspection plan should define:

- Critical areas and cosmetic zones
- Visual and dimensional acceptance
- Internal inspection where specified
- Plating or coating inspection
- Sampling and reaction criteria

No alloy ranks can replace a project-specific inspection plan. [Review porosity and inspection planning](/porosity-control-x-ray-inspection-castings/).

## RFQ Inputs for Zamak 3 or Zamak 5

Provide:

- 3D model and 2D drawing with GD&T
- Zinc alloy and material standard
- Expected annual volume and launch stages
- Load, wear and service-temperature requirements
- Post-casting forming or assembly operations
- Plating, coating and cosmetic requirements
- Inspection method and documentation scope
- Packaging, corrosion protection and project timing

Incomplete inquiries can still be reviewed. Missing requirements are identified before a material or production scope is confirmed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the main difference between Zamak 3 and Zamak 5?

Zamak 5 contains a higher intentional copper addition than Zamak 3. The difference generally favors higher strength and hardness in Zamak 5, while Zamak 3 is often selected where better ductility and dimensional stability are important.

### Is Zamak 5 always stronger than Zamak 3?

Zamak 5 is generally higher in strength and hardness, but the suitable alloy depends on the part's loads, forming operations, aging exposure, surface finish and assembly requirements. Published generic values are not a substitute for the approved material specification.

### When should Zamak 3 be considered?

Zamak 3 may be considered for complex thin-wall castings, parts requiring post-casting forming or applications where long-term dimensional stability and ease of finishing are important.

### When should Zamak 5 be considered?

Zamak 5 may be evaluated where higher strength, hardness or wear resistance is needed. Its lower ductility and dimensional-aging considerations should be reviewed against the application.

### What information is needed for a zinc die casting RFQ?

Provide the 3D model, 2D drawing with GD&T, alloy, expected volume, critical loads, wall sections, surface finish, machining requirements, inspection scope and any aging or corrosion requirements.

### Where should Zamak property values come from?

Use the governing zinc-alloy standard, approved material certificate and representative test data for the actual casting condition. Composition, mechanical properties and dimensional behavior should not be copied from an unsourced comparison table.

### How do Zamak castings compare with aluminum?

Zinc and aluminum die-casting alloys differ in density, melting range, castability, corrosion behavior, plating, machining and cost structure. The choice should be based on the complete part and production route rather than material price per kilogram.
