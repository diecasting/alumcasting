# Sand Casting Services for Prototype and Production Parts

Sand casting is a flexible route for forming complex or large metal components with an expendable sand mold. It can support prototype evaluation, bridge production and established production volumes when pattern equipment, molding, material and machining requirements are defined correctly.

[Request a Sand Casting Review](/contact/)

## When Sand Casting Fits a Project

Sand casting may be evaluated when:

- A prototype or low-to-medium volume does not justify high-volume permanent tooling
- The part has internal cavities or a geometry that is difficult to die cast
- The component is large or requires a substantial machining allowance
- Pattern changes may be needed during development
- The selected alloy and heat-treatment route suit the application

It is not automatically the lowest-cost or highest-precision process. Die casting, investment casting, permanent mold casting, fabrication or machining may be more suitable depending on the drawing and volume. Compare the process route against function, tolerance, finish and total project cost.

## Common Sand Casting Process Variants

Supplier quotations should identify the molding method rather than using “sand casting” as a generic label. Project-relevant variants can include:

- Green sand molding
- Chemically bonded sand molding
- No-bake molding
- Hand or floor molding
- Automated molding for suitable production volumes

The selected method affects pattern equipment, mold strength, surface condition, dimensional repeatability and production rate.

## Material and Heat-Treatment Review

Material selection begins with the customer specification. The review should confirm:

- Alloy designation and governing standard
- Chemical composition and traceability requirements
- Required casting condition
- Heat-treatment condition, if applicable
- Mechanical-property sampling
- Corrosion and finishing requirements

Do not assume that one nominal alloy designation defines every property. Production evidence should come from the approved chemistry, process route and test plan.

## Pattern and Tooling Considerations

Sand casting uses a pattern to form the mold cavity. Pattern cost and lead time depend on:

- Pattern size, complexity and expected life
- Material and surface requirements
- Core boxes and core assembly
- Draft and fillet requirements
- Shrink allowance
- Machining allowance
- Gating and risering design
- Handling and storage

The lowest initial pattern cost can lead to higher finishing or rework cost later. The quotation should explain the pattern scope and revision policy.

## Design Constraints

DFM review should cover:

- Minimum and transition wall sections
- Draft on vertical surfaces
- Fillets and sharp-corner reduction
- Thick masses and feeding distance
- Core placement and core support
- Machining stock and datum strategy
- Feature access for inspection
- Distortion during cooling and heat treatment

A geometry that can be molded may still be difficult to machine, inspect or finish economically. [Review tooling and DFM requirements](/die-casting-tooling/).

## Dimensional Control and Surface Finish

Achievable tolerances and surface finish are process- and supplier-specific. They depend on pattern equipment, molding method, alloy, geometry and the feature being measured.

The drawing should distinguish:

- Casting-only dimensions
- Machined dimensions and datums
- Critical sealing or bearing surfaces
- Draft and parting-line conditions
- Acceptable surface discontinuities
- Machining allowance

Final tolerances should be confirmed through a capability or first-article review rather than applied as a blanket value to every feature.

## Machining, Heat Treatment and Finishing

Sand castings often move to [CNC machining](/precision-cnc-machining/) for mounting faces, bores, threads, seal grooves and other critical features.

The process plan should establish:

- Casting datum and fixture strategy
- Machining allowance
- Heat-treatment sequence
- Stress relief or dimensional stabilization where specified
- Deburring, cleaning and inspection
- Coating, anodizing, painting, passivation or other approved finishing

Heat treatment must be specified for the alloy and required condition. It should not be added as a generic recommendation without reviewing distortion, property requirements and subsequent machining.

## Quality and Inspection Planning

Potential controls include:

- Incoming material verification
- Melt and pouring process records
- Pattern and mold checks
- First-article dimensional inspection
- Visual and surface inspection
- Radiographic or other internal inspection where specified
- Mechanical-property testing
- Final dimensional and functional checks

The inspection method, coverage, sampling and acceptance criteria must be agreed for the part. [Review porosity-control and X-ray planning](/porosity-control-x-ray-inspection-castings/).

## Prototype-to-Production Transition

A sand-cast prototype can provide early material and geometry validation, but a production process may require a different mold, tooling and inspection plan.

When reviewing the transition:

1. Confirm which prototype results are relevant to production.
2. Freeze the material, heat-treatment and critical requirements.
3. Compare tolerance, finish and volume with each candidate process.
4. Define new tooling and validation for any process change.
5. Update the inspection plan and acceptance criteria.

For EV housings and other die-cast applications, use [EV battery housing die-casting review](/ev-battery-housing-die-casting/) when the production intent is high-pressure die casting rather than sand casting.

- Inspection method, sampling and reports
- Packaging, traceability and delivery requirements

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is sand casting used for?

Sand casting uses an expendable sand mold and a pattern to form metal parts. It is considered for prototypes, low-to-medium volumes, large components and geometries that may not suit permanent die casting.

### Is sand casting suitable for small batch production?

It can be suitable when the pattern, molding method, machining allowance, material and delivery requirements fit the project. Tooling cost and lead time must be quoted for the actual pattern and production plan.

### What tolerances and surface finish can sand casting achieve?

Achievable dimensions and surface quality depend on the casting process variant, pattern equipment, alloy, part geometry, molding method and supplier capability. Critical features should be specified with a drawing and confirmed during feasibility review.

### Can sand castings be machined and finished?

Yes. Sand castings may require machining allowance for critical faces, holes and sealing features. Machining, heat treatment and finishing should be planned with the casting route because they affect datum strategy and final dimensions.

### How is porosity controlled in sand castings?

Porosity control can involve pattern and gating design, risering, melt quality, pouring practice and process control. Inspection method and acceptance limits must be defined for the part; no casting process guarantees zero porosity.

### What should an RFQ for sand casting include?

Provide the 3D model, 2D drawing with GD&T, alloy, quantity, pattern status, critical features, machining and finishing requirements, inspection scope and target delivery.
