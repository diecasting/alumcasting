# Automotive Aluminum Die Casting Parts

Aluminum die-casting support for automotive and EV component programs. AlumCasting reviews casting feasibility, tooling inputs, CNC post-machining and project-specific inspection requirements from the drawing and application data.

![Structural die-cast component with ribs and mounting features](/images/semi-solid-casting-ssm-zero-porosity-structural-parts.webp)

## Automotive and EV Component Families

Typical component families considered during project review include:

- **Powertrain and drivetrain:** housings, covers and mounting components.
- **E-mobility:** [aluminum motor housings](/die-cast-aluminum-electric-motor-housing-suppliers/), brackets and structural interfaces.
- **Thermal management:** [liquid-cooled aluminum cooling plates](/liquid-cooled-aluminum-cooling-plates-for-electric-vehicles/), housings and sealed assemblies.
- **Chassis and body:** structural brackets and other cast-and-machined interfaces.

Final process suitability depends on load case, geometry, alloy, wall sections, sealing requirements, machining scope and expected production volume.

## DFM Review Before Tooling

The DFM review connects the part design to the casting and machining plan. Inputs commonly include:

- 3D model and 2D drawing with GD&T.
- Aluminum alloy and required material condition.
- Critical structural, sealing and machining features.
- Nominal wall thickness and transitions.
- Draft angles, undercuts and slider requirements.
- Machining allowance, datums and fixture access.
- Expected annual volume and production stages.
- Inspection methods and required documentation.

This review identifies design and process questions before tooling details are released.

## Tooling and Trial Process

Tooling planning covers die concept, gating and thermal considerations, ejection, mechanical movements and production constraints. Trial planning should define the sample scope, inspection criteria and documentation attached to the trial or T0 stage.

Tooling and trial timing is confirmed after the part, die concept, material and trial scope are reviewed. No fixed sample lead time is published here.

## Casting and CNC Post-Machining

Casting and machining requirements should be planned together:

- Machine selection is confirmed against part size, die geometry, alloy and shot weight.
- CNC scope is defined from finished datums, fixtures, machining allowance and tolerances.
- Cast surfaces, machining access and inspection access are reviewed during DFM.
- Final dimensional capability is verified against the approved drawing and inspection method.

CNC post-machining and other manufacturing services are reviewed per project scope.

## Finishing Requirements

Surface treatment, coating and other finishing requirements are reviewed with the application environment, appearance requirements and downstream assembly process.

The exact finishing method and production source are confirmed during project review and RFQ.

## Inspection and Documentation Scope

Inspection planning is based on part function, critical characteristics and customer requirements. Depending on the project, requirements may include:

- X-ray inspection for internal porosity where selected by the approved inspection plan.
- CMM dimensional verification.
- Pressure or leak testing for sealed components.
- Visual, material and process documentation.
- First-article or production inspection records when specified.

Acceptance criteria, inspection coverage and report format are agreed before production.

## Supplier Qualification and RFQ Inputs

Quality-system certificates, applicable scope and supporting project documents should be verified during supplier qualification.

For an automotive RFQ, include:

- 3D model and 2D drawing with GD&T.
- Alloy and material condition.
- Expected annual volume and launch timing.
- Critical walls, structures, sealing faces and machining features.
- Surface-finishing and assembly requirements.
- Inspection, traceability and documentation requirements.

## Technical FAQ: Automotive Die Casting

### Why is porosity control important for automotive die-cast parts?

Porosity can affect pressure tightness, fatigue performance and machining quality in automotive components. Acceptable porosity depends on the critical area, inspection method, sampling plan and acceptance criteria agreed for the specific part.

### What information is needed for an automotive DFM review?

Provide the 3D model, 2D drawing with GD&T, aluminum alloy, annual volume, critical wall sections, draft requirements, sealing areas, machining allowance and any required inspection or documentation scope.

### Which aluminum alloys are commonly considered for automotive die casting?

A380 and ADC12 are common choices for die-cast housings and high-fluidity components. A356 may be evaluated when higher elongation or heat-treatment compatibility is required. Final alloy selection depends on the design, process route and validation requirements.

### How are inspection requirements determined?

Inspection methods, critical characteristics, sampling and documentation are defined from the drawing and project requirements. Depending on the approved plan, this may include X-ray, CMM dimensional verification and pressure or leak testing.

### Can a die casting be machined after casting?

Yes. Machining allowance, datums, fixture access and finished tolerances should be reviewed together with the casting design. Feasibility and inspection requirements are confirmed for the specific cast and machined features.
